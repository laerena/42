- operations
	swap top 2
		sa: swap top 2 numbers of stack A
		sb: swap top 2 numbers of stack B
		ss: do sa and sb

	rotate (shift up)
		ra: move the top of A to bottom
		rb: move the top of B to bottom
		rr: rotate both at once

	push top number from one to another
		pb: push top of A to top of B
		pa: push top of B to top of A

	reverse rotate (shift down)
		rra: move the bottom of A to top
		rrb: move the bottom of B to top
		rrr: move the bottom of A&B to its top

- radix: O(n log n)
	sort numbers by looking at one digit at a time, from least important to most important
	partitioning, not comparing
	comparisons are expensive in stack world, partitioning is cheap

	characteristics:
	deterministic, mechanical, bit-based, O(n log n)
	does NOT think about position.
	does NOT try to minimize rotations.
	does NOT use stack geometry.

	algorithmically elegant, but not smart in movement optimization

	operators for radix - binary operators
	>>
	(x >> k) move all bits of x to the right by k positions - sliding bits right, the rightmost bits fall off
   		x = 101100
    	x >> 1 = 010110
    	x >> 2 = 001011
	&
	((...) & 1) extracts the last bit(the least significant bit)

	eg. binary of 5 = 101
		5 >> 1 = 101 -> 10 = 2 integer division by 2
		5 >> 2 = 101 -> 1 = 1  integer division by 4
		5 >> 3 = 0             integer division by 8
		5 >> k				   integer division by 2^k
		max >> bits is max / (2^bits)

	((x >> k) & 1) check bit k

	eg. 6 binary is 110
    	((6 >> 0) & 1) = 0
    	((6 >> 0) & 1) = 1
    	((6 >> 0) & 1) = 1
    	radix gets these and sorts

- chunking
	"heuristic divide-and-reassemble algorithm over a constrained state machine"

	pushes values in controlled ranges
	uses rotation direction to minimize moves
	extracts largest values from B efficiently

	filtering values: scan A in circles, everytime something in current chunk range, send to B


- complexity
	if I double the amount of data, how much more work do I have to do?
	eg. O(n)       look at each number once
    	O(n²)      for every number, compare it to every other number
    	O(n log n) split the list in half, solve each half, then combine


not important/required for this version of push_swap but interestingly:
	- How messy is this list?
		Disorder = mistakes / total relationships
		eg. A = [1, 2, 3, 4] (perfect order)
    		disorder = 0

    		A = [4, 3, 2, 1] (worst order)
    		disorder = 1

    		A = [3, 1, 2]
    		mistakes: 3 before 1, 3 before 2
    		mistakes = 2
    		possible comparisons = 3

    		disorder = 2/3 ~= 0.66 (high)
